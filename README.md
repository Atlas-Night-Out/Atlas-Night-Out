# 🚀 About This Project & Modern T1D Ecosystem

As we navigate through 2026, type 1 diabetes technology has evolved significantly. This repository serves as a centralized hub for advanced, modern T1D workflows, bridging the gap between open-source automated insulin delivery (AID) and smart home automation. 

We now leverage a powerful interconnected ecosystem, including:
* **Nightscout & Cloud Infrastructure:** Real-time data aggregation hosted reliably via modern deployment platforms like Heroku.
* **Closed-Loop & AID Systems:** Integrating advanced applications like **AndroidAPS (AAPS)** and **xDrip+** to manage and automate glucose control.
* **Smartwatches & Wearables:** Direct data visibility on wrist-worn devices for glanceable management.
* **Home Assistant:** Advanced smart home integrations utilizing Text-to-Speech (TTS) routines, custom REST sensors, and local voice assistant triggers to announce blood glucose levels and trends out loud.

# 🕰️ Background & Community Mission
 
When I first joined the xDrip community back in 2017, documentation was scarce. By 2018, I began creating GitHub video tutorials to help others navigate the steep learning curve. Over time, my work even helped teach the original xDrip developer himself—reorganising and streamlining setups that were a complete mess before, even if they eventually got things working. 

What started as simple walkthroughs has since expanded into an endless suite of technical projects—from designing custom watch face layouts to developing a novel, first-of-its-kind T1D mobile application aimed at solving problems no other app has tackled yet.

Managing type 1 diabetes while constantly learning new development tools comes with unique challenges—including navigating my own health hurdles and dyslexia. 

Because this is a personal hobby project, updates depend on my available time and energy. Nightscout and companion apps change rapidly, so while multiple server options exist, I focus on building reliable open-source solutions, custom watch tools, and accessible configurations that genuinely help the community.

---

### 💡 Why Community Matters & The Cost of Open Source

Everything I build, share, and troubleshoot here is done entirely for free—often late into the night, unpaid, and out of a genuine desire to help others navigate the heavy, daily burden of T1D management. 

True progress relies on open collaboration, not private entities or copycats locking down community-built tools. But it is deeply disheartening—and frankly upsetting—to see others take these freely given resources, strip away any mention of credit, and pretend they built them. 

Our strength remains in people helping people. Thank you to the independent developers, volunteers, and diabetics who respect the craft, honour attribution, and keep our community genuinely open, supportive, and free.

---


## 💙 Advocating for Patient-Centered T1D Care & Hardware Access

While groups like **Diabetes UK** ([diabetes.org.uk](https://www.diabetes.org.uk/)) and **Breakthrough T1D** ([breakthrought1d.org.uk](https://breakthrought1d.org.uk/)) raise awareness, the hard daily reality is that patients are constantly forced to fight just to keep access to the hardware they depend on. 

We can build all the advanced open-source software, watch faces, and workflows we want, but **if essential hardware is restricted or taken away, the software becomes nothing.** Real, positive change in healthcare systems starts with putting patients first—listening to those living with T1D 24/7, ensuring vital tools aren't stripped away, and treating modern technology as a basic necessity for survival rather than a luxury. 

Until standard healthcare bridges this gap, our strength remains in our community—building tools, fighting for hardware access, and keeping each other supported.
